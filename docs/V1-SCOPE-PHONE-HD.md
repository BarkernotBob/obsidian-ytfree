## What was measured (2026-11-04, Isaiah's Mac, signed out, `tools/hls-probe.mjs`)

| Video | IOS HLS manifest | Other clients |
|---|---|---|
| `aqz-KE-bpKQ` | none | WEB/MWEB refused, TV/ANDROID_VR bot check |
| `dQw4w9WgXcQ` | **yes: 144p to 2160p, 1080p H.264 served at 0s, 60s, 90s, 180s and the end** | same |
| `9bZkp7q19f0` | none | same |
| `LXb3EKWsInQ` | none | same |
| `8jPQjjsBbIc` | none | same |

- The IOS client's HLS manifest is the one HD source the phone can reach that serves the whole
  video. It exists on about 1 video in 5 (also the July measurement: 1 in 10).
- googlevideo sends **no CORS header** on the manifest or its segments. On an iPhone with iOS 17.1+
  the player hands HLS to hls.js, which fetches with XHR from `capacitor://localhost` and is
  refused. So even on the videos that have the manifest, the phone could not play it. iOS's own
  player (`<video src=…m3u8>`) does not need CORS.
- The ANDROID/IOS separate files stop at about a minute (#25), so the stitched HD path fails on the
  phone for most videos.
- Making web clients serve the whole file needs an anti-bot (PO) token generated by running
  YouTube's BotGuard check. Claude's safety check blocks building that (bot-check circumvention),
  so it is out of scope.

## Part 1 — built

1. **iOS plays YouTube's HLS natively on the phone.** On a phone, an HLS stream that is not a
   stitched one goes to the `<video>` element directly instead of through hls.js. iOS picks the
   height itself (up to 1080p or more) and switches as the network changes.
2. **HLS first on the phone.** The phone's HD lookup asks IOS first and takes its manifest at once
   when there is one, before spending requests on the separate files that stop at a minute.
3. **Badge** shows the height iOS is actually playing (read from the picture's size).
4. **If native HD fails**, the player drops to 360p at the same spot (same rule as stitched HD).
5. Desktop is unchanged.

Expected effect: HD on the phone for the videos that have an IOS manifest (roughly 1 in 5); the
rest stay at 360p, as now.

## Part 2 — option, needs Isaiah's call: HD through the Mac at home

The Mac already gets whole-video HD links from yt-dlp. Those links are locked to the network's
public address, so a phone on the same home Wi-Fi can use them.

- The desktop plugin runs a small HD lookup service on the home network while Obsidian is open on
  the Mac (off by default, a setting turns it on, a shared code in settings pairs the phone).
- The phone, with HD on, asks the Mac for a video's yt-dlp links, then streams the bytes from
  YouTube itself, stitched exactly as the desktop does now.
- Needs: the Mac awake with Obsidian open, both on the same network. Away from home the phone
  falls back to Part 1 / 360p. iOS will ask once for Local Network permission.
- Not measured yet: whether Obsidian iOS's `requestUrl` can reach a plain `http://` address on the
  LAN. That is the first thing to check if this is picked.

## Out of scope

- PO token / BotGuard (see above).
- A per-video quality switch in the player (#15, separate thread).

## Manual test (Part 1, for Isaiah)

1. Install the build (Claude installs it into the vault). On the iPhone, wait for iCloud to sync,
   then Settings → Community plugins → turn YT Free off and on.
2. Settings → YT Free: "HD on this phone (experimental)" is on.
3. Open a note or Preview for `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQw4w9WgXcQ` and tap Play. Within a
   few seconds the badge on the picture should read 720p or 1080p, and the picture sharpens.
4. Let it play past 1:30, then drag to near the end. It keeps playing in HD, no fall back to 360p.
5. Open any other video, e.g. `aqz-KE-bpKQ`. It plays at 360p and the badge says no HD, as before.
